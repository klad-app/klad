# Shapes, paths and text

Shapes, paths and text live on vector layers. Add one with "vector" in the Layers pane. Their points stay editable, they stay sharp at any size, and they can be filled with gradients.

## Draw rectangles and ellipses

Press U and drag. Shift makes a square or a circle, alt draws from the centre, and rectangles can have rounded corners. The fill is the current colour and the outline the second one, at the width in the bar over the canvas.

## Draw paths

Press P. Click to place a corner, drag to pull out a curve, click the first point to close the path, Return to finish it open, Esc to drop it. Closed paths are filled and outlined, open ones outlined; "fill" and "outline" in the bar turn either off.

## Change points

Press A and click the shape. Drag points and handles; alt click on the outline adds a point, alt click on a point switches it between corner and smooth, alt drag on a handle breaks it. A rectangle or an ellipse becomes a path the moment you change a point, and "simplify the path" turns a dense traced outline into a few smooth points.

## Colour a shape

Select the shape with V. The bar next to it fills it with the current colour or outlines it in the current colour. For a gradient, switch the fill in layer settings to linear or radial and edit its stops.

## Write text

Press T, click and type. Return starts a new line and Esc finishes. Double click existing text with V or T to edit it in place.

The font picker shows each family in its own face: about fifty open fonts come with klad, and a search finds any family on Google Fonts and downloads it. The font is copied into the drawing with its licence, so the text looks the same on every machine. "add a font" in the File menu puts a font file of your own in the drawing.

## Repeat, scatter and mirror a shape

Select a shape and use "repeat it" (copies along a line, an arc or a grid), "scatter it" (copies strewn over an area, each sized and turned from a seed) or "mirror it" from the Layer menu. They stay settings you can change, and "take the shapes out" turns them back into plain shapes.
